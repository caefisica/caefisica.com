$$\begin{aligned}
\langle XX^T\rangle &=
\begin{bmatrix}
\langle x_1^2\rangle & \langle x_1x_2\rangle & \cdots\\
\langle x_1x_2\rangle & \langle x_2^2\rangle & \cdots\\
\vdots & \vdots &
\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}\tag{17}$$

these look close to the entries of $\Theta$ in (10). what we need is to subtract $\langle x_i\rangle\langle x_j\rangle$. Thus, we finally conclude that the covariance matrix may be written as

$$\begin{aligned}
\Theta &= \langle XX^T\rangle - \langle X\rangle\langle X\rangle^T
\end{aligned}\tag{18}$$

This is a very convenient way of writing down the covariance matrix. If you ever get confused about the vector notation, then think about the problem in terms of components

$$\begin{aligned}
\Theta_{ij} &= \langle x_i x_j\rangle - \langle x_i\rangle\langle x_j\rangle
\end{aligned}\tag{19}$$

which is just Eq (10) written down explicitly.
