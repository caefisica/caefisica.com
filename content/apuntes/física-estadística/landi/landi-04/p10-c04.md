The mean vector may be arbitrary but the covariance matrix must satisfy two properties. First, it must be symmetric, $\Theta^T=\Theta$. And second, it must be \underline{positive definite}; i.e., all its eigenvalues must be larger than zero. This is actually a consequence of Eq.(9) [It is something which is a bit more difficult to demonstrate].

\underline{Technical comment:} any positive definite matrix may be factorized as in (27), which is known as the \underline{Cholesky decompo-}
\underline{sition} of the matrix. A pos. def. matrix is the matrix analogue of a positive number and the factorization (27) is the matrix analogue of taking the square root.
