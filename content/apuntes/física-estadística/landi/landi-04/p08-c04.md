where $\mu_i$ and $q_{ij}$ are arbitrary coefficients. Now $x_1$ and $x_2$ are no longer independent

$$\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Cov}(x_1,x_2) &= \operatorname{Cov}(q_{11}z_1+q_{12}z_2+\mu_1,\ q_{21}z_1+q_{22}z_2+\mu_2)\\
&= q_{11}q_{21}\operatorname{Cov}(z_1,z_1)+q_{12}q_{22}\operatorname{Cov}(z_2,z_2)\\
&= q_{11}q_{21}+q_{12}q_{22}
\end{aligned}\tag{22}$$

By mixing the $z_i$ we produce correlated variables. As a limiting case we may take $x_1=z_1$ and $x_2=z_1$. In this case $x_1$ and $x_2$ are perfectly correlated.
