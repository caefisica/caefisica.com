Thus, the distribution of $v$, $\theta$, $\phi$ will be according to (25) and (57):

$$\begin{aligned}
p(v,\theta,\phi)&=\left(\frac{m}{2\pi k_BT}\right)^{3/2}v^2\sin\theta\,e^{-mv^2/2k_BT}
\end{aligned}\tag{63}$$

This is the joint distribution to observe the velocity with magnitude $v$ and at a certain direction $\theta,\phi$.

Notice that it is uniform in $\phi$ (it does not depend on $\phi$).

It is not uniform in $\theta$ since it depends on $\sin\theta$. This makes it more likely to observe values of $\theta$ near the equatorial line.

The r.v.s, $(v,\theta,\phi)$ are statistically independent since

(63) factors into a product

$$\begin{aligned}
p(v,\theta,\phi)&=\underbrace{\frac{1}{2\pi}}_{P(\phi)}
\underbrace{\frac{\sin\theta}{2}}_{P(\theta)}
\underbrace{4\pi\left(\frac{m}{2\pi k_BT}\right)^{3/2}v^2e^{-mv^2/2k_BT}}_{P(v)}
\end{aligned}\tag{64}$$
