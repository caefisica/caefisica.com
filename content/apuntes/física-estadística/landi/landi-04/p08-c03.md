We say the vector $\begin{bmatrix}z_1\\z_2\end{bmatrix} \sim N(0,I_2)$, which means a multivariate normal with zero mean and covariance matrix $I_2$. It is straightforward to extend these ideas to an arbitrary number of $z_i$'s.

The $z_i$ are uncorrelated, which is not very interesting. We may produce correlated normal r.v.s by taking linear combinations of $z_1$ and $z_2$

$$\begin{aligned}
x_1 &= q_{11}z_1 + q_{12}z_2 + \mu_1\\
x_2 &= q_{21}z_1 + q_{22}z_2 + \mu_2
\end{aligned}\tag{21}$$
