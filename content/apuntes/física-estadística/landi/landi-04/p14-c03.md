This formula may be extended to more complicated transfor-
mations. It always has the same structure.

For instance, let $x,y$ be two r.v.'s with joint PDF $P_{x,y}(x,y)$ and let $z=f(x,y)$. Then

$$\begin{aligned}
P_z(z) &= \int dxdy\ \delta(z-f(x,y))\ P_{x,y}(x,y)
\end{aligned}\tag{31}$$

You may demonstrate this formula using the same trick that we used to demonstrate (39) (via the characteristic function).

Or suppose you want to change variables, from $x,y$ to $z=f(x,y)$ and $w=g(x,y)$. In this case

$$\begin{aligned}
P_{z,w}(z,w) &= \int dxdy\ \delta(z-f(x,y))\delta(w-g(x,y))\ P_{x,y}(x,y)
\end{aligned}\tag{32}$$

If we integrate (32) over $w$ we get (30).
