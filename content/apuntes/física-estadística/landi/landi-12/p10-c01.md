$$\langle\sigma_n\sigma_{n+r}\rangle=\frac{1}{Z}\sum_{\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_N}\sigma_n\sigma_{n+r}e^{\beta J(\sigma_1\sigma_2+\cdots+\sigma_{N-1}\sigma_N)}$$

To compute the correlation function it is easier to use the $\tau$’s. Since $\sigma_n^2=1$, we may write

$$\begin{aligned}
\sigma_n\sigma_{n+r}&=(\sigma_n\sigma_{n+1})(\sigma_{n+1}\sigma_{n+2})\cdots(\sigma_{n+r-1}\sigma_{n+r})\\
&=\tau_{n+1}\cdots\tau_{n+r}
\end{aligned}$$

The correlation function then becomes

$$\langle\sigma_n\sigma_{n+r}\rangle=\frac{1}{2(2\cosh J)^{N-1}}\sum_{\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_N}(\tau_{n+1}\cdots\tau_{n+r+1})e^{\beta J(\tau_2+\cdots+\tau_N)}$$

The sum is now completely factored. The sum over $\tau_1$ cancels the 2 downstairs and the sums that do not have $\tau_n$ multiplying the exponential will cancel the cosh downstairs. What survives are $r$ sums of the form

$$\frac{1}{2\cosh\beta J}\sum_\tau\tau e^{\beta J\tau}=\frac{1}{2\cosh\beta J}(e^{\beta J}-e^{-\beta J})=\tanh\beta J$$

Hence, we find

$$\begin{aligned}
\langle\sigma_n\sigma_{n+r}\rangle&=\tanh^r(J/T)
\end{aligned}\tag{3.7}$$
