Let us look more closely at the matrix

$$n \cdot \sigma = n_x \sigma_x + n_y \sigma_y + n_z \sigma_z = \begin{pmatrix} n_z & n_x - i n_y \\ n_x + i n_y & -n_z \end{pmatrix}$$

It is straightforward to verify that the eigenvalues of this matrix are $\pm 1$. Hence, the eigenvalues of $A$ in Eq. $(3.23)$ are

$$\begin{aligned} a_0 \pm a \end{aligned}\tag{3.24}$$

As for the eigenvectors, it is convenient to parametrize $n$ in spherical coordinates,

$$\begin{aligned}
n_x &= \sin \theta \cos \phi \\
n_y &= \sin \theta \sin \phi \\
n_z &= \cos \theta
\end{aligned}$$

Then the eigenvectors may be written as

$$\begin{aligned}
|v_+\rangle &= \begin{pmatrix} \cos \theta/2 \\ e^{i\phi} \sin \theta/2 \end{pmatrix}
&
|v_-\rangle &= \begin{pmatrix} -e^{-i\phi} \sin \theta/2 \\ \cos \theta/2 \end{pmatrix}
\end{aligned}\tag{3.25}$$

This procedure gives a systematic way to find eigenvalues and eigenvectors of $2 \times 2$ matrices. And the resulting formulas are much more convenient to work with. Every time I have to deal with $2 \times 2$ matrices, I always use this procedure.
