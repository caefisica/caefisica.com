Magnetization

Let us look at

$\langle\sigma_1\rangle=\frac{1}{Z}\sum_{\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_N}\sigma_1V(\sigma_1,\sigma_2)V(\sigma_2,\sigma_3)\ldots V(\sigma_N,\sigma_1)$

The quantity $\sigma_1V(\sigma_1,\sigma_2)$ may be interpreted as another matrix, $V'$. It has values [cf. Eq. (3.14)]

$V'=\begin{pmatrix}e^{\beta(J+h)}&e^{-\beta J}\\-e^{-\beta J}&-e^{\beta(J-h)}\end{pmatrix}$

So we may also write

$\langle\sigma_1\rangle=\frac{1}{Z}\operatorname{tr}(V'V^{N-1})$

But you may verify that

$V'=\sigma_zV$

Hence, we have

$\langle\sigma_1\rangle=\frac{1}{Z}\operatorname{tr}(\sigma_zV^N)$

This logic also extends to the magnetization at any other site. In general,

$$\begin{aligned}
\langle\sigma_n\rangle=\frac{1}{Z}\operatorname{tr}(V^{n-1}\sigma_zV^{N-n+1})
\end{aligned}\tag{3.29}$$

But using the cyclic property of the trace we find that

$$\begin{aligned}
\langle\sigma_n\rangle=\frac{1}{Z}\operatorname{tr}(\sigma_zV^N)
\end{aligned}\tag{3.30}$$

The cyclic property of the trace thence reflects the translational invariance of the model. We may compute this trace in the $|v_\pm\rangle$ basis which diagonalizes $V$. We then get

$\langle\sigma_n\rangle=\frac{1}{Z}\left\{\langle v_+|\sigma_z|v_+\rangle\lambda_+^N+\langle v_-|\sigma_z|v_-\rangle\lambda_-^N\right\}$

In the thermodynamic limit, however, $Z=\lambda_+^N$ so the term containing $\lambda_-$ vanishes. We are then left with

$$\begin{aligned}
\langle\sigma_n\rangle=\langle v_+|\sigma_z|v_+\rangle
\end{aligned}\tag{3.31}$$
