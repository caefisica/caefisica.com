Some properties of Pauli matrices

Computing the eigenvalues of $V$ in Eq. $(3.14)$ is an easy task since the matrix is $2 \times 2$. However, to compute correlation functions we also need the eigenvectors, which are usually more cumbersome to deal with, even for $2 \times 2$ matrices. Whenever you are faced with a problem like this, I always recommend you use Pauli matrices. The idea is to use the fact that the most general $2 \times 2$ matrix may be written as

$$\begin{aligned}
A &= a_0 + \mathbf{a} \cdot \boldsymbol{\sigma}
\end{aligned}\tag{3.22}$$

for four numbers $a_0$, $a_x$, $a_y$ and $a_z$. Here $\mathbf{a} \cdot \boldsymbol{\sigma}$ is an abbreviation for $a_x\sigma_x + a_y\sigma_y + a_z\sigma_z$. We may also let $a = |\mathbf{a}|$ and introduce the unit vector $\mathbf{n} = \mathbf{a}/a$. Then

$$\begin{aligned}
A &= a_0 + a(\mathbf{n} \cdot \boldsymbol{\sigma})
\end{aligned}\tag{3.23}$$

The idea of using Pauli matrices is to exploit the following properties:

1. The eigenvectors of $A$ are the same as those of $\mathbf{n} \cdot \boldsymbol{\sigma}$.

2. If $\lambda$ is an eigenvalue of $\mathbf{n} \cdot \boldsymbol{\sigma}$, then $a_0 + a\lambda$ is an eigenvalue of $A$.
