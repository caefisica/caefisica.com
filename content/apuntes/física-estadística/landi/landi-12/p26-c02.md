Next we turn to the susceptibility:

$$\begin{aligned}
\chi &= \left.\frac{\partial m}{\partial h}\right|_{h=0}
\end{aligned}\tag{3.53}$$

We may obtain a formula for $\chi$ close to the critical point by differentiating both sides of Eq. (3.51). This gives

$$1=-(T_c-T)\frac{\partial m}{\partial h}+Tm^2\frac{\partial m}{\partial h}$$

or,

$$\begin{aligned}
\chi(T)&=\frac{1}{(T-T_c)+Tm^2}
\end{aligned}\tag{3.54}$$

This formula is illustrated in Fig. 3.15. As can be seen, the susceptibility diverges around the critical point. Above the critical point $m=0$ and Eq. (3.54) simplifies to

$$\begin{aligned}
\chi&=\frac{1}{T-T_c}
\end{aligned}\tag{3.55}$$

which is known as the Curie-Weiss law. Recall that for paramagnets we used to have $\chi=C/T$. In the present case, even though at $T>T_c$ the material behaves like a paramagnet, we still see some reflections of the interactions, which modify Curie’s law to $C/(T-\theta)$. In our present model $\theta=T_c$. In real materials we find that both are usually different. For $T<T_c$ we substitute Eq. (3.47) to find

$$\begin{aligned}
\chi&=\frac{1}{2(T_c-T)}
\end{aligned}\tag{3.56}$$

It also diverges with the same exponent and a slightly different pre-factor.
