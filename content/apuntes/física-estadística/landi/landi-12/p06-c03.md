Figure 3.4: Probability that the system is fully magnetized as a function of temperature and the number of particles, Eq. (3.6).

Let us compute the probability that all spins are up. The probability for any state $\sigma=(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_N)$ is, of course,

$P_\sigma=\frac{e^{-\beta E_\sigma}}{Z}$

When all spins are up, $\sigma_1\sigma_2+\ldots+\sigma_{N-1}\sigma_N=(N-1)$, so

$$\begin{aligned}
P(\mathrm{all\ up})&=\frac{e^{\beta J(N-1)}}{2(\cosh\beta J)^{N-1}}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{e^{\beta J}}{\cosh\beta J}\right)^{N-1}
\end{aligned}\tag{3.6}$$

By symmetry, this is the same as the probability that all spins are down. This result is plotted as a function of $T$ in Fig. 3.4 for several values of $N$. As can be seen, when $N$ is moderately small, $P(\mathrm{all\ up})$ changes gradually from a small value at high $T$ to $1/2$ at $T=0$. However, we see that as we increase $N$ the probability of finding all spins up tends to a step function, being zero above a certain temperature and exactly $1/2$ below.
