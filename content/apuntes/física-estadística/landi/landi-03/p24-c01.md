The diffusion equation

Eq (59) actually corresponds to the continuum limit, where each step of the random walk is very tiny, but takes place in a small time interval.

You may verify that $p(y,t)$ satisfies the diffusion equation

$$\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial p}{\partial t} &= -\mu\frac{\partial p}{\partial y}+\frac{\sigma^2}{2}\frac{\partial^2p}{\partial y^2}
\end{aligned}\tag{60}$$

The first term is called the drift term and the second term is the diffusion term.

we can check that the solutions of (60) in Eq (59) directly.

or we can pretend we do not know (59) and try to derive it. That is most easily done using (again) the characteristic function.

$$\begin{aligned}
p(y,t) &= \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} ds\,e^{-isy}G(s,t)
\end{aligned}\tag{61}$$
