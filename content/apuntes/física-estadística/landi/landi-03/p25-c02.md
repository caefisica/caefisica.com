Combining (62) - (64) into (60) we then get

$$\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}ds\,e^{-isy}\frac{\partial G}{\partial t}
&=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}ds\,e^{-isy}\left\{isyG-\frac{s^2\sigma^2}{2}G\right\}
\end{aligned}$$

the equality must also hold for the integrands due to the uniqueness of the Fourier transform. Thus

$$\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial G}{\partial t}&=\left\{isy-\frac{s^2\sigma^2}{2}\right\}G
\end{aligned}\tag{65}$$
