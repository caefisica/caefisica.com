This is already a polynomial in $s$ so we conclude that the  
Normal has only two non-zero cumulants; the mean $\mu$ and  
the variance $\sigma^2$. All higher order cumulants are zero.

This property is absolutely unique of the Normal and, as we  
will see soon, it is the reason why it is such an important  
distribution.

If we take the limit $\sigma \to 0$ we obtain the $\delta$-function  
distribution $\delta(x-\mu)$, with CGF $C(s)=is\mu$. Thus, the $\delta$-  
function has only one non-zero cumulant.

The $\delta$ has 1 cumulant, the normal has 2. Can we come up  
with a distribution that has 3, 4, 5 or some other finite  
number of cumulants? The answer is no:

A distribution either has 1 cumulant (the $\delta$), 2  
cumulants (the normal) or an infinite number of  
cumulants (all other distributions)

Reason: it can be shown that any CGF which is a polynomial  
in $s$ of order larger than two will lead to a PDF  
that cannot be positive everywhere.
