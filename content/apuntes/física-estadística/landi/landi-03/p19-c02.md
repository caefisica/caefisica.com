$$\begin{aligned}
\langle Y\rangle &= \langle X\rangle\\
\operatorname{var}(Y) &= \frac{\operatorname{var}(X)}{N}
\end{aligned}\tag{42}$$

Before we were talking about $N$ "large". We now see that if $N\to\infty$ the second cumulant will also vanish and only the mean survives. We may write this mathematically as

$$\begin{aligned}
\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{x_1+\cdots+x_N}{N}=\langle x\rangle
\end{aligned}\tag{43}$$

This is known as the law of large numbers: the arithmetic mean of a sufficiently large number of random variables tend to the actual mean. In this case $y$ becomes a $\delta$-distribution.

These results explain why in thermodynamic experiments we are able to measure deterministic quantities. In principle the response of the system should be a random variable, but the number of particles is so large that, by the law of large numbers, we end up getting a deterministic result
