$$\begin{aligned}
q_m &= \sum_u Q_u e^{i\omega_u t}
\end{aligned}\tag{65}$$

The variables $Q_u$ and $q_m$ are Fourier transforms of each other. The variable $Q_u$ satisfies the differential equation

$$\begin{aligned}
\ddot{Q}_u &= -\omega_u^2 Q_u
\end{aligned}\tag{66}$$

which is the equation of a harmonic oscillator with frequency $\omega_u$.

what we have just done is known as the normal mode decomposition. We started with a problem of $N$ variables $q_m$, which interact with each other [Eq (46)]. Then we made a transformation to a new set of coordinates $Q_u$ [Eq (65)] in such a way that these new variables are completely independent of each other. The $Q_u$ are the normal modes of the system.
