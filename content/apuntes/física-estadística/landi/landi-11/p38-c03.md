We then get a wave equation

$$\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial t^2}&=c^2\frac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial x^2}
\end{aligned}\tag{71}$$

In the continuum limit the discrete fn are replaced by  
a continuous field $\phi(x,t)$, which satisfies a wave equation.  
this in the theory of elasticity : we describe deformations in a  
body by a continuous field $\phi(x,t)$ describing the displacement  
at position $x$ and time $t$.

If we plug in a plane-wave solution $e^{i(kx-\omega t)}$ in eq (71)  
we will find that it is a solution only if $\omega = c|k|$. Thus, we  
conclude that when we take the continuum limit we wash  
away the high $k$ physics and keep only the long wavelength  
(less energy) phenomena.

The phonon picture can also be made to appear here. Now  
the phonons are the elementary excitations of the displacement  
field, just like photons are the excitations of the electromag-  
netic fields and electrons are the excitations of the Dirac  
field. This is quantum field theory. It unifies all areas of  
physics by interpreting elementary particles and quasi-particles  
as the elementary excitations of quantum fields.
