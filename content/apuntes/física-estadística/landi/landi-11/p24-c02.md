we now group the $A_m$ into a vector

$$\begin{aligned}
\vec{A}&=\begin{pmatrix}A_1\\A_2\\\vdots\end{pmatrix}
\end{aligned}\tag{49}$$

then we define a matrix, for instance of $N=5$

(Example with $N=5$)

$$\begin{aligned}
W&=\frac{q}{m}
\begin{pmatrix}
2&-1&0&0&-1\\
-1&2&-1&0&0\\
0&-1&2&-1&0\\
0&0&-1&2&-1\\
-1&0&0&-1&2
\end{pmatrix}
\end{aligned}\tag{50}$$

notice the solitary $(-1)$'s in the corners; they come from the periodic boundary conditions.

we may now write (48) as

$$\begin{aligned}
W\vec{A}&=\omega^2\vec{A}
\end{aligned}\tag{51}$$

this is an eigenvalue/eigenvector Eg. we started with (45), which had as unknowns $\omega$ and $A_m$. we now see that $\omega^2$ is simply the eigenvalue of $W$ and $A_m$ are the eigenvectors. we have reduced the solution of Newton's equations to a linear algebra problem.
