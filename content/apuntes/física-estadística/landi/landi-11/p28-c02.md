$$e^{i(k+2\pi/a)x_m}=e^{ikx_m}\underbrace{e^{i(2\pi/a)ma}}_{e^{2\pi im}=1}$$

So we only need to take eigenvectors with $k$ within an interval  
of length $2\pi/a$. The actual interval is arbitrary, but the  
most popular choice is

$$\begin{aligned} k &\in \left[-\frac{\pi}{a},\frac{\pi}{a}\right] \end{aligned}\tag{55}$$

In condensed matter we call this the first Brillouin zone.

we therefore take

$$\begin{aligned} k &= \frac{2\pi\ell}{Na}, \qquad \ell=-\frac{N}{2},-\frac{N}{2}+1,\ldots,\frac{N}{2}-1,\frac{N}{2} \end{aligned}\tag{56}$$

$\ell$ falls on $N$ distinct values in the interval $-\frac{N}{2}<\ell\le\frac{N}{2}$. This  
gives us the $N$ distinct eigenvectors $\vec{A}_k$, with components

$A_{k,m}=B e^{ikx_m},$

But what about $B$? well, it must follow from the  
normalization condition of the eigenvectors

$$1=\sum_{m=1}^{N}|A_{k,m}|^2=\sum_{m=1}^{N}|B|^2=N|B|^2$$
