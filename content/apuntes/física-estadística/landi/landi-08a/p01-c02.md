In thermodynamics this is called the Gibbs entropy. In probability theory it goes by the name of Shannon entropy and they define it without the $k_B$.

The entropy is a measure of the quadratic of the distribution.

To see that, note how $S$ is a sum of functions like $-p \ln p$ where $p \in [0,1]$. this function looks like this

[graph: vertical axis labeled $-p\ln p$, horizontal axis labeled $p$, with the curve rising from $(0,0)$ to a maximum and falling to $(1,0)$]

It is zero when $p=0$ or $p=1$. Thus, if we have a deterministic or elemenni-
mistic distribution, $P_m=1$ and $P_m=0$ for $m\ne m$, the entropy will be zero

$$\begin{aligned}
S=0 \quad \text{for a deterministic}\\
\text{distribution}
\end{aligned}\tag{2}$$
