$$\begin{aligned}
\frac{\hbar^2}{2I} &\simeq 0.00023\ \mathrm{eV}
\end{aligned}\tag{32}$$

we therefore see that the typical rotational energies are much smaller than the typical vibrational energies. Moreover, they are also much smaller than $k_BT \sim 0.026\ \mathrm{eV}$, so at room temperature there will be a significant amount of energy stored in the rotations and practically none stored in the vibrations
