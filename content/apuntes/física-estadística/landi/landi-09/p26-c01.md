The heat capacity of the QHO is similar to (46)

$$\begin{aligned}
C &= k_B\left(\frac{\hbar\omega}{k_BT}\right)^2 \cdot \frac{e^{\hbar\omega/k_BT}}{\left(e^{\hbar\omega/k_BT}-1\right)^2}
\end{aligned}\tag{49}$$

Plugging our value we get $C \sim 0.027$. [You get the same value if you use (46)]. Thus we see that at $T=300K$ the vibration of the diatomic molecules also contribute but negligibly to the specific heat [Remember from our tables that $C_{CO} \sim 2.5 k_B$, whereas the kinetic energy only contributes $1.5$].
