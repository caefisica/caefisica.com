Please note that "m" here means a generic description of  
the quantum numbers. So you should always sum over  
all quantum numbers. For instance, in the case of the  
Hydrogen atom we would have

$$\begin{aligned}
z &= \sum_{m_l,m_s} e^{-\beta E_m}
\end{aligned}\tag{5}$$

Even though the energy depends only on $m_l$, we sum over  
all states [remember: $z = z$ instead summe $e$ sum  
over states].
