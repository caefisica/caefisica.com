thus, we conclude that

$$\begin{aligned}
\chi &= \frac{1}{k_B T}\left[\langle\mu^2\rangle-\langle\mu\rangle^2\right]
\end{aligned}\tag{56}$$

just like the heat capacity is actually the variance of the energy, we see now that the susceptibility $\chi$ is actually the variance of $\mu$.

A nice feature of Eq (56) is that it allows us to compute $\chi$ even at $B=0$ (even though $\chi = \partial M/\partial B$).
