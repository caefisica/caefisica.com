Binomial converges to the Poisson

Let $X \sim \operatorname{Bin}(m,p)$ and assume that $m \to \infty$ but $p \to 0$, in such a way that $\lambda = mp$ remains fixed. that is, There are many trials, but the chance of success in each is small.

Substitute $p = \lambda/m$ in the distribution $P(X=k)$ of the Binomial, Eq (12), we get

$$\begin{aligned}
P(X=k) &= \binom{m}{k}p^k(1-p)^{m-k}
= \frac{m!}{k!(m-k)!}\frac{\lambda^k}{m^k}\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{m}\right)^{m-k}
\end{aligned}$$
