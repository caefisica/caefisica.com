when $m\to\infty$ we then get

$$\begin{aligned}
\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{m}\right)^{-k}&\to 0\\
\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{m}\right)^m&\to e^{-\lambda}
\end{aligned}$$

the last limit is the definition of the exponential (if you leave the base in the banks, it grows exponentially). we therefore grow money in the banks, it grows exponentially.

e.g.

$P(x=k)\simeq e^{-\lambda}\frac{\lambda^k}{k!}$

thus, when $m\to\infty$, $p\to0$ but $\lambda=np$ remains fixed, the Binomial converges to the Poisson. this is the origin of Bawson's paradigm.
