$$e^\lambda=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^k}{k!}$$

Differentiate both sides with respect to $\lambda$

$$e^\lambda=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k\frac{\lambda^{k-1}}{k!}$$

This is almost (27), we are just missing a $\lambda$, so multiply by $\lambda$ on both sides:

$$\begin{aligned}
\lambda e^\lambda&=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k\frac{\lambda^k}{k!}
\end{aligned}\tag{28}$$

we have explained the missing $\lambda$ Now we have exactly (27). Thus

$$\langle x\rangle=e^{-\lambda}\lambda e^\lambda=\lambda$$

which is (26).
