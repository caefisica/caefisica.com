Poisson paradigm

the Poisson distribution appears approximately whenever  
you are analyzing the number of "successes" of a very  
large number of trials, where each trial has a very  
small chance of success.

An important example is radioactive decay. Consider  
a sample full of radioactive atoms and suppose we are  
measuring the number of decays per minute. the prob. that  
a given atom decays during that minute is very small.  
But there are many many atoms, so you get some  
finite result.

If you knew that, on average $\lambda=\langle x\rangle$ atoms decay  
per minute, then $x$ will be approximately $\mathrm{Pois}(\lambda)$.

As another example, let $x$ denote the number of goals  
in a world cup match. The prob. that one player scores  
is very small, but there are many players, so $x\sim\mathrm{Pois}(\lambda)$  
(approximately, of course). We know that the average  
number of goals in $\langle x\rangle=2.5$. If we assume that $x\sim\mathrm{Pois}(\lambda)$  
then we may estimate the full distribution

$$\begin{aligned}
P(k\text{ goals in a match}) &= e^{-2.5}\frac{(2.5)^k}{k!}
\end{aligned}\tag{29}$$
