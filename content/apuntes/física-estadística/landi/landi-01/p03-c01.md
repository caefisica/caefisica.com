Figuring out what are the probabilities is usually a difficult task, the prime source of information is experiment. There are also some approximation techniques, like assuming the dice at corners are equally likely or assuming that the uncertainties in an experiment follow a Gaussian distribution. But that is pretty much it.

The most remarkable fact of the discipline we call statistical mechanics is that, for certain classes of systems, we can infer their probabilities from first principles. That is, we can start with the fundamental laws of physics, such as Newton's laws or Schrödinger's equations, and we may deduce what the probabilities will be, without having to perform the experiment.

This is something which is absolutely unique about physics. You will not find it anywhere else.
