The mean/average

the prob distribution contains all the information about  
a r.v. But sometimes we want just a few numbers to  
get a rough description. the most important such number  
is the mean :

$$\begin{aligned}
\langle x\rangle &= \sum_\nu \nu P_\nu = \sum_\nu \nu P(x=\nu)
\end{aligned}\tag{17}$$

Please note that different books use different notations

$$\begin{aligned}
\langle x\rangle &= E(x) = \bar{x}
\end{aligned}\tag{18}$$

Example: $x\sim\mathrm{Bern}(p)$

$\langle x\rangle = \sum_\nu \nu P_\nu = (0)P_0 + (1)P_1 = (0)(1-p)+(1)p$

$$\begin{aligned}
x\sim\mathrm{Bern}(p):\quad \langle x\rangle &= p
\end{aligned}\tag{19}$$

this is the coolest feature about Bern(p): the mean is the actual  
probability.

the mean satisfies the following properties

$$\begin{aligned}
\langle cx\rangle &= c\langle x\rangle
\end{aligned}\tag{20}$$
