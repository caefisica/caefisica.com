The second term in the sum of all pair-wise interactions

[Experimentally, we find that 3-body interactions are negligib-
le], the sum is restricted over $j<i$ in order to avoid
double counts. We can remove this constraint by dividing
by 2:

$$\begin{aligned}
H &= \sum_{i=1}^{N} \frac{p_i^2}{2m} + \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j} V(\vec r_i,\vec r_j)
\end{aligned}\tag{70}$$

the potential $V(\vec r_i,\vec r_j)$ could be Van der Waals forces between
the molecules, or they could describe hard-core repulsion. A
typical $V$ will only depend on the distance $r_{ij}=|\vec r_i-\vec r_j|$
between two particles. And it will usually look like this

For large $r$ the interaction is attractive, but if they get too
close their electron clouds will touch each other and the
interaction becomes repulsive.
