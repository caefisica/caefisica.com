Notice how this will factor, as before, in a position term and a momentum term. Thus, we conclude that, even when the particles interact, the momentum $p_i$ of the particles will continue to be statistically independent, with distribution

$$P(p_i)=\frac{e^{-\beta p_i^2/2m}}{(2\pi m k_B T)^{3/2}}\tag{71}$$

[see Eq (35)]. This is quite a remarkable fact and is related to the fact that our theory is classical. In the quantum formalism this is no longer true.

The partition function also factors as

$$\begin{aligned}
Z&=\int e^{-\beta H}\,d^3r_1\ldots d^3r_N\,d^3p_1\ldots d^3p_N\\
Z&=(2\pi m k_B T)^{3N/2}\int d^3r_1\ldots d^3r_N\exp\left\{-\frac{\beta}{2}\sum_{i,j}V(r_i,r_j)\right\}
\end{aligned}\tag{72}$$

If you are ever able to compute this integral, let me know. You will surely win a Nobel prize for it. This integral contains all the information about an interacting system of molecules. It therefore contains all the information about phase transitions from gas to liquid and then from liquid to solid.
