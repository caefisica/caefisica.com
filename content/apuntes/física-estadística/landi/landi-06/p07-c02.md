$P(y_i) = \frac{e^{\alpha_i H_i(y_i)}}{z_i}$ \tag{15}

where $z_i$ and $\alpha_i$ are some constants. If we now look at (11) we

get

$P(y_i)P(y_2) = \frac{e^{\alpha_1 H_1(y_1) + \alpha_2 H_2(y_2)}}{z_1 z_2}$ \tag{16}

we see that our ansatz (15) will only work if $\alpha_1 = \alpha_2$. Thus

$P(y_i) = \frac{e^{\alpha H_i(y_i)}}{z_i}$ \tag{17}

where $\alpha$ is a constant that must be the same for all systems

in the same heat bath.

With some reflection it is possible to conclude that (17)

is actually the most general function satisfying (11). Any

other function that you come up may be recast in the

form of Eq. (17) by a suitable redefining of $\alpha$ and $C_i$.

It is also worth noting that (17) is a direct consequence of

the postulate of equal a priori probabilities, Eqs (8) or (9).
