we already know that: $\frac{Z'}{Z}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\ln Z=-\langle H\rangle$. As for the fourth term, we have

$\frac{Z''}{Z}=\frac{1}{Z}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial\beta^2}\int e^{-\beta H}Dy=\frac{1}{Z}\int H^2e^{-\beta H}Dy=\langle H^2\rangle$

Thus, we reach the remarkable result;

$$\begin{aligned}
c &= \frac{\partial U}{\partial T}=\frac{1}{k_BT^2}\left[\langle H^2\rangle-\langle H\rangle^2\right]
\end{aligned}\tag{9.0}$$

the heat capacity is actually proportional to the variance of the energy. It is a measure of the fluctuations of $H$ due to the contact with a heat bath.
