The Gibbs formalism models the contact of a system with a heat bath

$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Heat bath: a very large system at a constant temperature}\\
T\text{ (I always imagine a bucket of water)}
\end{aligned}\tag{6}$$

the bath must always be much larger than the system. So if the “system” is a DNA molecule, a glass of water is already very large. But if the system is a ping-pong ball, then perhaps it is better to use a bath tub.

the contact between the system and the bath will cause the system to change randomly. The molecules in the bath will continually collide with the system and exchange energy with it. Consequently, the microstate $y$ of the system will be a random variable.

This process is precisely a Markov chain: the microstate $y$ will keep on jumping from one possible state to the other (in this case both time and $y$ are continuous, but the idea is the same).
