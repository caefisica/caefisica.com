The Free energy

we have seen that when $H$ is a sum of independent terms,  
$Z$ will be a product. Consequently $\ln Z$ will also be a sum.  
It is convenient to define a new quantity, which we call  
the Helmholtz free energy, as

$$\begin{aligned}
F &= -k_B T \ln Z
\end{aligned}\tag{79}$$

Thus, $F$ will be an extensive quantity

$$\begin{aligned}
\text{If}\quad H &= H_1+\ldots+H_N \longrightarrow F=F_1+\ldots+F_N
\end{aligned}\tag{80}$$

The term “free energy” comes from thermodynamics. We  
will understand later on why this energy is “free”.

From Eq (50) we see that $U$ and $F$ are related by

$U=-\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\ln Z=-\frac{\partial T}{\partial\beta}\frac{\partial}{\partial T}\ln Z=k_BT^2\frac{\partial}{\partial T}\ln Z=-T^2\frac{\partial}{\partial T}(-k_B\ln Z)$

or

$$\begin{aligned}
U &= -T^2\frac{\partial}{\partial T}\left(\frac{F}{T}\right)
\end{aligned}\tag{81}$$

This relation is also found from thermodynamics, where it  
is called the Gibbs - Helmholtz relation.

we may also relate $F$ to the heat capacity ($C$), with some  
simplifications, we find

$$\begin{aligned}
C &= \frac{\partial U}{\partial T}=-T\frac{\partial^2F}{\partial T^2}
\end{aligned}\tag{82}$$
