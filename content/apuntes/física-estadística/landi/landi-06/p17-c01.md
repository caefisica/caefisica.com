For instance, in (45) we found that

$Z_N = V^N (2\pi m k_B T)^{3N/2} = V^N \left[\frac{2\pi m}{\beta}\right]^{3N/2}$

$$\begin{aligned}
\ln Z_N &= \ln\left[V^N(2\pi m)^{3N/2}\right]-\ln\beta^{3N/2}\\
&=\ln\left[V^N(2\pi m)^{3N/2}\right]-\frac{3N}{2}\ln\beta
\end{aligned}\tag{50}$$

the first term does not depend on $\beta$ so

$U=-\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\ln Z=\frac{3N}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\ln\beta=\frac{3N}{2}\frac{1}{\beta}$

or

$U=\frac{3N}{2}k_BT$

which is precisely Eq (46).

Eq (50) is very useful. I always recommend you use it. Notice that it also matches well with the idea of Eq (44): if

$Z_N=Z_1^N$

then

$U=-\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\ln Z_N=-N\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\ln Z_1=NU_1$

If the $Z$ are products, then $U$ must be a sum.
