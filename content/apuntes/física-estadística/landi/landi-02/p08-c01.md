Connection between the exponential and the Poisson

Now I want to show you an interesting interpretation  
of the exponential distribution. Let $N_t$ be the number  
of buses which go through a bus stop during a time $t$.  
It is reasonable to assume that

$$\begin{aligned}
N_t &\sim \mathrm{Pois}(\lambda t)
\end{aligned}\tag{22}$$

where $\lambda$ = "average number of buses per hour" (assuming $t$  
is measured in hours).

Now suppose you just arrived at the bus stop. Let $T$ denote  
the time while the first bus arrives. I will show now that

$$\begin{aligned}
T &\sim \mathrm{Expo}(\lambda)
\end{aligned}\tag{23}$$

The basis of this calculation is the statement

$$\begin{aligned}
P(T>t) &= P(N_t=0)
\end{aligned}\tag{24}$$

The LHS is the prob. that the first bus only arrives after $t$. The RHS  
is the prob that the number of buses which passed up to time $t$  
is zero. There are just two ways of saying the same thing.

Now, $N_t \sim \mathrm{Pois}(\lambda t)$, so

$P(N_t=k)=e^{-\lambda t}\frac{(\lambda t)^k}{k!}$
