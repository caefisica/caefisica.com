You may also think about $\delta(x)$ as a “limiting” case of  
other distributions, with $\sigma \to 0$

$$\begin{aligned}
X\sim\operatorname{Unif}\left(-\frac{\sigma}{2},\frac{\sigma}{2}\right):\quad
\delta(x)\simeq
\begin{cases}
\frac{1}{\sigma}, & -\frac{\sigma}{2}\le x\le\frac{\sigma}{2}\\
0, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}\tag{37}$$

“very sharp uniform”

or

$$\begin{aligned}
X\sim N(0,\sigma):\quad
\delta(x)\simeq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}e^{-x^2/2\sigma^2}
\end{aligned}\tag{38}$$

“very sharp Gaussian”

or

$$\begin{aligned}
X\sim C(0,\sigma):\quad
\delta(x)\simeq\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\sigma}{\sigma^2+x^2}
\end{aligned}\tag{39}$$

Cauchy distribution. We will learn about it later.
