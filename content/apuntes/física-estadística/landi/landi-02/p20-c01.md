Expressing discrete r.v.s using a PDF

Let $X$ be a r.v. with probabilities $P(X=k)$. We may  
also interpret $X$ as a continuous random variable, with

PDF

$$\boxed{p(x)=\sum_k P(X=k)\,\delta(x-k)}\tag{42}$$

this allows us to treat discrete and continuous r.v.s using  
a unified formalism. Normalization now translates as

$$\begin{aligned}
1&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}p(x)\,dx
=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left[\sum_k P(X=k)\,\delta(x-k)\right]dx\\
&=\sum_k P(X=k)\underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\delta(x-k)\,dx}_{=1}
\end{aligned}$$

thus

$$1=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}p(x)\,dx=\sum_k P(X=k)\tag{43}$$

In the description (42) a discrete r.v. is therefore  
interpreted as if it varied continuously, but with a  
PDF that has extremely sharp peaks.
