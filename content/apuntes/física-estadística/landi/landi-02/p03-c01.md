The computations of moments is analogous to the discrete

case

$$\begin{aligned}
\langle x\rangle &= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x p(x)\,dx
\end{aligned}\tag{5}$$

or, if $f(x)$ is an arbitrary function of $x$, then

$$\begin{aligned}
\langle f(x)\rangle &= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)p(x)\,dx
\end{aligned}\tag{6}$$

Another important quantity, is the cumulative distribution function, CDF. It is defined as

$$\begin{aligned}
F(x) &= P(x\leq x) = \int_{-\infty}^{x}p(y)\,dy
\end{aligned}\tag{7}$$

The CDF is an actual prob.; ie, it is restricted to $[0,1]$. It is also a monotonically increasing function of $x$, starting at $F(-\infty)=0$ and ending at $F(\infty)=1$.

From (7) and the fundamental Theorem of calculus we have that

$$\begin{aligned}
p(x) &= \frac{dF}{dx}
\end{aligned}\tag{8}$$
