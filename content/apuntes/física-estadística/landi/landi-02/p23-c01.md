1) when $y=f(x)$ is one-to-one

$$\begin{aligned}
P_Y(y)&=P_X(x)\left|\frac{dx}{dy}\right| \quad \text{with } x=f^{-1}(y)
\end{aligned}\tag{44}$$

Example: $X\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2)$ and $Y=aX+b$.

$\left|\frac{dx}{dy}\right|=\frac{1}{|a|}$ and $x=\frac{y-b}{a}$

Thus

$$\begin{aligned}
P_Y(y)&=P_X(x)\frac{1}{|a|}
=\frac{1}{|a|}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}
e^{-\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}}\\
&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}|a|\sigma}
\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\left(\frac{y-b}{a}-\mu\right)^2\right\}\\
&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}|a|\sigma}
\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2(a\sigma)^2}(y-b-a\mu)^2\right\}
\end{aligned}$$

This is the PDF of a Gaussian again. thus we conclude that

$$\begin{aligned}
\text{If }X\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2)\text{ then }aX+b\sim N(a\mu+b,|a|^2\sigma^2)
\end{aligned}\tag{45}$$

As a particular case, if $Z\sim N(0,1)$ then $X=\sigma Z+\mu\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2)$

which is (32), (33).

Another interesting consequence is

$$\begin{aligned}
\text{If }X\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2)\text{ then }-X\sim N(-\mu,\sigma^2)
\end{aligned}\tag{46}$$
