distribution

$$\begin{aligned}
z \sim N(0,1) :\quad p(z) = \frac{e^{-z^2/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}
\end{aligned}\tag{27}$$

The normalization is ensured by the  
famous Gaussian integral

$$\begin{aligned}
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-ax^2}\,dx = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{a}}
\end{aligned}\tag{28}$$

setting $a = 1/2$ gives $\sqrt{2\pi}$.

The mean is

$$\begin{aligned}
\langle z\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} z e^{-z^2/2}\,dz = 0
\end{aligned}\tag{29}$$

I know it is zero because of parity: this is the product of an  
even with an odd function
