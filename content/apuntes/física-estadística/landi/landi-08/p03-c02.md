$$\begin{aligned}
Z(\lambda) &= \sum_M e^{-\beta E_M(\lambda)}
\end{aligned}\tag{3}$$

Everything else depends on $T$, but $T$ is fixed, so we don't worry about it.  
[ The same calculation works for classical system, in exactly  
the same way. Just replace sums by integrals ].

the internal energy of the system is

$$\begin{aligned}
u(\lambda,T) &= \sum_M E_M(\lambda)P_M(\lambda)
\end{aligned}\tag{4}$$

Now suppose the external agent changes $\lambda$ slightly to $\lambda + d\lambda$.  
This will push the system away from equilibrium. But at the  
same time the system and the bath will readjust and  
equilibrium will again be reestablished.  
The change in the internal energy of the system will be

$$
du = u(\lambda+d\lambda,T)-u(\lambda,T)=\sum_M d(E_M P_M)
$$
