Fermi gas at finite temperatures

we saw that at $T=0$ all states up to $E_F$ were occupied ; the  
Fermi sphere is filled, At $T\ne0$ thermal fluctuations will allow  
some of the electrons to occupy states above the Fermi surface.  
For $T\ne0$ the average number of electrons at an energy $\epsilon$ will  
be given by the Fermi-Dirac distribution

$$\bar{n}(\epsilon)=\frac{1}{e^{\beta(\epsilon-\mu)}+1}\tag{18}$$

Let's understand the behaviour of this function at $T=0$ first.  
then we move to $T\ne0$. For $T\to0$ (or $\beta\to\infty$) we have

$$\begin{aligned}
e^{\beta(\epsilon-\mu)} &=
\begin{cases}
\gg 1 & \epsilon>\mu\\
\ll 1 & \epsilon<\mu
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}\tag{19}$$
