Once we know the Fermi momentum, we may determine the  
Fermi energy as

$$\begin{aligned}
E_F &= \frac{\hbar^2 k_F^2}{2m} = \frac{\hbar^2}{2m}(3\pi^2 n)^{2/3}
\end{aligned}\tag{4}$$

we may also determine other quantities of interest. For instance,  
since momentum is $\hbar k$ and velocity is $p/m$, we may define  
the Fermi velocity as

$$\begin{aligned}
v_F &= \frac{\hbar k_F}{m} = \frac{\hbar}{m}(3\pi^2 n)^{1/3}
\end{aligned}\tag{5}$$

This gives a rough measure of the typical velocities of the electrons  
in the highest occupied states.
