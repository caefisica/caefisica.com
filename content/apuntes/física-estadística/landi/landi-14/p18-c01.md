what we see is that at $T=300\ K$, $\bar n(e)$ will differ from  
$\theta(e_F-e)$ only in the vicinity of the Fermi surface. the electrons  
deep within the Fermi sphere practically do not feel the effects of  
the thermal fluctuations. All that the thermal fluctuations do  
is produce a small cloud of electrons above the Fermi surface.

All thermodynamic formulae are modified simply by introdu-  
cing $\bar n(e)$; for instance

$$\begin{aligned}
N &= \int_0^\infty de\ D(e)\ \bar n(e)
\end{aligned}\tag{24}$$

$$\begin{aligned}
U &= \int_0^\infty de\ D(e)\ e\ \bar n(e)
\end{aligned}\tag{25}$$

we may interpret $D(e)\bar n(e)$ as a thermally weighted density  
of states
