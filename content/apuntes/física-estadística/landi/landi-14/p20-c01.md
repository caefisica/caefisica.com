Now let us apply the Sommerfeld expansion to (24) and (25) :

$$\begin{aligned}
N &= \int_0^\infty d\epsilon\,D(\epsilon)\bar n(\epsilon) \simeq \int_0^\mu d\epsilon\,D(\epsilon) + \frac{\pi^2}{6}(k_B T)^2D'(\mu)
\end{aligned}\tag{30}$$

$$\begin{aligned}
U &= \int_0^\infty d\epsilon\,D(\epsilon)\epsilon\bar n(\epsilon) \simeq \int_0^\mu d\epsilon\,D(\epsilon)\epsilon + \frac{\pi^2}{6}(k_B T)^2\left[D(\mu)+\mu D'(\mu)\right]
\end{aligned}\tag{31}$$

Now we need to be careful with the difference between $\epsilon_F$ and $\mu$. For instance, we have seen that

$$\begin{aligned}
N &= \int_0^{\epsilon_F} d\epsilon\,D(\epsilon)
\end{aligned}\tag{32}$$

But this does not mean that

$$N \ne \int_0^\mu d\epsilon\,D(\epsilon) \qquad \text{(wrong!)}$$

there are two different things. In fact, we will learn in

a second that

$$\mu \simeq \epsilon_F-\alpha(k_B T)^2$$

where $\alpha>0$ is a constant.

Let us then be careful and do our expansions so that they

always come out correct to order $(k_B T)^2$. This means that

$$\frac{\pi^2}{6}(k_B T)^2D'(\mu) \simeq \frac{\pi^2}{6}(k_B T)^2D'(\epsilon_F)$$

and

$$\frac{\pi^2}{6}(k_B T)^2\left[\mu D'(\mu)+D(\mu)\right] \simeq \frac{\pi^2}{6}(k_B T)^2\left[\epsilon_FD'(\epsilon_F)+D(\epsilon_F)\right]$$

In these terms we may safely replace $\mu$ by $\epsilon_F$ since any

corrections will be of order higher than $(k_B T)^2$.
