Ferm

$$\begin{aligned}
j &= (k,\sigma)\\
k_i &= \frac{2\pi \ell_i}{L},\quad \ell_i=0,\pm1,\pm2,\ldots\\
\sigma &= \pm1\\
\boxed{E_k=\frac{\hbar^2 k^2}{2m}}
\end{aligned}\tag{1}$$

Let us now understand how the Power principle affects the  
ground state of the system.

Suppose our box has 1 electron. the ground state then  
corresponds to placing this single electron in the lowest  
energy state, which is that with $k=(0,0,0)$.

$gs:\ 1e^- @ (0,0,0,\pm1)$ \hfill $E=0$

Since there is no magnetic field, $\sigma=+1$ and $\sigma=-1$ are  
degenerate, so it does not matter in which state we put the  
electron.

Now consider two electrons. we construct the gs as

$gs:\ (0,0,0,1),\ (0,0,0,-1),$ \hfill $E=0$
