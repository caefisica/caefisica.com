T_F &= \frac{\epsilon_F}{k_B}

Note, in contrast to this, that the energy per electron in a classical ideal gas, $\frac{3}{2}k_BT$, vanishes at $T=0$ and achieves a value as large as (2.32) only at $T=\frac{2}{5}T_F\approx 10^4\ \mathrm{K}$.

Given the ground-state energy $E$, one can calculate the pressure exerted by the electron gas from the relation $P=-\left(\frac{\partial E}{\partial V}\right)_N$. Since $E=\frac{3}{5}N\epsilon_F$ and $\epsilon_F$ is proportional to $k_F^2$, which depends on $V$ only through a factor $n^{2/3}=(N/V)^{2/3}$, it follows that$^{16}$

$$\begin{aligned}
P &= \frac{2}{3}\frac{E}{V}.
\end{aligned}\tag{2.34}$$

One can also calculate the compressibility, $K$, or bulk modulus, $B=1/K$, defined by:

$$\begin{aligned}
B &= \frac{1}{K}=-V\frac{\partial P}{\partial V}.
\end{aligned}\tag{2.35}$$

Since $E$ is proportional to $V^{-2/3}$, Eq. (2.34) shows that $P$ varies as $V^{-5/3}$, and therefore

$$\begin{aligned}
B &= \frac{5}{3}P=\frac{10E}{9V}=\frac{2}{3}n\epsilon_F.
\end{aligned}\tag{2.36}$$

or

$$\begin{aligned}
B &= \left(\frac{6.13}{r_s/a_0}\right)^5\times 10^{10}\ \mathrm{dynes/cm^2}.
\end{aligned}\tag{2.37}$$
