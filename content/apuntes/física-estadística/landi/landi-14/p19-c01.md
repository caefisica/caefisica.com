<u>Sommerfeld expansion</u>

Integrals such as (24) and (25) usually cannot be computed analytically because of $\bar{n}(\epsilon)$. But that is not such a big deal since the deviation of $\bar{n}(\epsilon)$ from $\Theta(E_F-\epsilon)$ is usually small, the <u>Sommerfeld expansion</u> is a way of expanding $\bar{n}(\epsilon)$ at low temperatures.

The mathematics of the Sommerfeld expansion is not so easy, but the idea is. Consider an integral of the form

$$\begin{aligned}
\int_0^\infty d\epsilon\, f(\epsilon)\,\bar{n}(\epsilon)d\epsilon
\end{aligned}\tag{28}$$

we have already seen that $\bar{n}(\epsilon)$ differs only slightly from $\Theta(E_F-\epsilon)$:

I will not carry out the Sommerfeld expansion here.  
It is done in all detail in Appendix C of Ashcroft and Mermin.  
the result is

$$\begin{aligned}
\int_0^\infty f(\epsilon)\,\bar{n}(\epsilon)d\epsilon \simeq \int_0^\mu f(\epsilon)d\epsilon+\frac{\pi^2}{6}(k_B T)^2f'(\mu)
\end{aligned}\tag{29}$$

If $T\to0$ then $\mu\to E_F$ and we recover all zero temperature results, such as (26)
