The 'basic assumption of a Markov chain is the, so called, Markov property:

$$\begin{aligned}
P(X_{t+1}=m_{t+1}\mid X_t=m_t,\ X_{t-1}=m_{t-1},\ \ldots,\ X_0=m_0)
= P(X_{t+1}=m_{t+1}\mid X_t=m_t)
\end{aligned}\tag{2}$$

Think of "t" as now: this property says that the future depends only on the current state and not on past events. We say Markovian systems have a very short memory (like fish).

The quantities

$$\begin{aligned}
Q_{mn}=P(X_{t+1}=n\mid X_t=m)=\operatorname{Prob}(m\to n)
\end{aligned}\tag{3}$$

are called the transition probabilities. They represent the probability of observing a transition from $m\to n$.

Suppose now that we knew $P(X_t=m)$. We may then find $P(X_{t+1}=n)$ using the law of total probability

$$\begin{aligned}
P(X_{t+1}=n)
&=\sum_m \overbrace{P(X_{t+1}=n\mid X_t=m)}^{Q_{mn}}P(X_t=m)
\end{aligned}\tag{4}$$

Let us switch to a more compact notation:

$$\begin{aligned}
P_m(t)=P(X_t=m)
\end{aligned}\tag{5}$$

then Eq (4) may be written as

$$\begin{aligned}
P_n(t+1)=\sum_m Q_{mn}P_m(t)
\end{aligned}\tag{6}$$

This is the basic formulae of a Markov chain. Note that this is a dynamical equation, It is a recipe for how to evolve your system in time (like Newton's law).
