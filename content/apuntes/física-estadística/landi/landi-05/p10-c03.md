we see that, as time passes, the probabilities tend to "settle down" at certain values. This is what we call the steady-state distribution

when the system reaches the steady-state, evolving it further will not change anything. So, if we let $\vec{p}^{*}$ denote the probabilities in the steady-state, then

$$\begin{aligned}
Q\vec{p}^{*} &= \vec{p}^{*}
\end{aligned}\tag{17}$$

This is the equation determining the steady-state. Anyone who has studied linear algebra before will recognize this as an eigenvalue/eigenvector product. The steady-state probability vector is the eigenvector of $Q$ with eigenvalue $1$. Thus, finding the steady-state is reduced to a linear algebra problem.
