<u>A silly example</u>

Suppose that the weather may either be sunny or rainy.

If it is sunny today, then there is a 20% chance that it will be raining tomorrow (since water evaporates). IF it is rainy today, then there is a 40% chance that it will be sunny tomorrow.

Please note that, in reality, the weather does <u>not satisfy</u> the Markov property (2). It's memory is certainly much better.

we may represent our silly process by a graph

$$\begin{aligned}
S &\xrightarrow{0.2} R, & R &\xrightarrow{0.4} S,\\
S &\xrightarrow{0.8} S, & R &\xrightarrow{0.6} R
\end{aligned}\tag{1.0}$$

this is a 2-state system. We may set "1" = "Sunny" and "2" = "Rainy".

then our system will be described by a 2-component vector

$$\vec{p}(t)=
\begin{bmatrix}
P(x_t=S)\\
P(x_t=R)
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
p_1(t)\\
p_2(t)
\end{bmatrix}$$

using the law of total probability we get

$$\begin{aligned}
P(x_{t+1}=S)
&=\underbrace{P(x_{t+1}=S\mid x_t=S)}_{0.8}P(x_t=S)
+\underbrace{P(x_{t+1}=S\mid x_t=R)}_{0.4}P(x_t=R)\\
P(x_{t+1}=R)
&=\underbrace{P(x_{t+1}=R\mid x_t=S)}_{0.2}P(x_t=S)
+\underbrace{P(x_{t+1}=R\mid x_t=R)}_{0.6}P(x_t=R)
\end{aligned}$$
