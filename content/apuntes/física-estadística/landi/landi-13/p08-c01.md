Fock space

All we just said also generalizes to a system of $N$ particles. But writing down these symmetrized and anti-  
symmetrized wave functions becomes quite cumbersome when $N$ is large.

There is a simpler description, called the Fock space representation, where we simply say how many particles are in each quantum state, ignoring completely the question of which particle is in which.

From now on I will use the following notation:

$$\boxed{\begin{aligned}
j &= \text{single particle state}\\
\text{example: } &(k,\sigma)
\end{aligned}}\tag{17}$$

the Fock representation of a many particle state is then given by a set of numbers

$$\begin{aligned}
\{m_j\} &= \{m_1,m_2,\ldots\}
\end{aligned}\tag{18}$$

where

$$\begin{aligned}
m_j &= \text{number of particles in}\\
&\text{state }j
\end{aligned}\tag{19}$$
