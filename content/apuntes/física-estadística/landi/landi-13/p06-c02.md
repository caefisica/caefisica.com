this is the \underline{Pauli exclusion principle}: two identical fermions  
can never occupy the same quantum state.

So far I have ignored spin; the actual quantum state of  
an electron is given by 4 quantum numbers; $(k,\sigma)$, where $\sigma=\pm1$.

thus, we can have two electrons with momentum $k$, as long  
as they have opposite spins. Two electrons with the same $k$ and  
the same $\sigma$ is not possible by the Pauli principle.

As another illustration, consider the energy levels of an  
electron in a hydrogen atom. these levels (orbitals) are  
characterized by four quantum numbers $(n,l,m_l,\sigma)$.

The ground state corresponds to $n=1$, $l=0$ and $m_l=0$.  
It therefore has two possible states: $(1,0,0,+1)$ and $(1,0,0,-1)$.

In a He atom we may put one electron in each state. this  
is the $1s^2$ electronic configuration.
